About the El Niño GHG Dashboard

A Spark Climate Solutions tool for watching how the carbon cycle responds to El Niño conditions: surface CO₂ at long-running monitoring stations, satellite column CO₂, vegetation greenness anomalies and active fires on one map. All data on this page are embedded at build time rather than fetched live; the table below gives each layer’s latest date, and the page is rebuilt periodically from the scripts in its GitHub repository.

How the station values are aggregated

  • ICOS: all ICOS values here are from in-situ continuous analysers (cavity ring-down or FTIR) in the ATC Level 2 and NRT products — no flask or EM27/SUN column data are included, so there is no overlap with the COCCON layer. Each station is tagged by platform (tall tower ≥ 50 m inlet, mountain ≥ 1500 m, otherwise surface/coastal) and whether it also runs an ICOS flask programme. Hourly CO₂ (and CH₄, where measured) from the highest sampling inlet at each station is averaged into calendar-month means. Level 2 release data are used wherever they exist; the NRT growing series fills months after the latest release. Only values with the ICOS “good” quality flag are included, and a month needs at least 120 valid hours (≈5 days) to be kept. Where several data objects overlap, the Level 2 value wins.
  • NOAA: monthly means are used exactly as published by GML (flagged samples already excluded by NOAA). Sites whose last published month is before 2024 are omitted as inactive. For Mauna Loa only, the flask record (to December 2025) is extended with NOAA’s preliminary in-situ monthly means from the GML trends page (January–September 2026); these are shown dashed in the chart and may be revised. Preliminary data are not published for the other sites, and the official in-situ monthly files also end in December 2025.
  • Anomaly (default colouring): latest monthly mean minus the mean of the same calendar month over the previous five years. At least three of those five years must exist; otherwise the station is grey. Because atmospheric CO₂ grows about 2.5 ppm per year and CH₄ about 10 ppb per year, this anomaly is positive almost everywhere for those gases; it answers “how much higher than the recent past”, not “is this unusual”. Each measurement type has its own colour scale (CO₂ 0–12 ppm, CH₄ 0–60 ppb, PM₂.₅ ±20 µg/m³).
  • Trend-adjusted anomaly: a straight line is fitted to the previous five years’ values for the same calendar month and extrapolated to the latest year; the anomaly is the latest value minus that expectation. Positive means above the station’s recent growth path.
  • ENSO sensitivity: for each station and gas, the 12-month growth rate (month minus the same month a year earlier) is regressed on the monthly-mean Niño 3.4 anomaly shifted by 0–12 months; the lag with the largest absolute correlation is reported with its slope (growth change per °C of Niño 3.4). It uses the whole embedded record (2015 onward for CO₂/CH₄), so the 2015–16 and 2023–24 events dominate. Sites with fewer than 36 months of growth data are excluded.
  • Zonal anomaly (OCO-2, PM₂.₅, CO): each cell minus the mean of its latitude band (2° for OCO-2 and PM₂.₅, 3° for CO) for the same window or month. This removes the secular trend and the north–south gradient without needing prior-year files; it is not an anomaly against a climatology, and over the oceans it mostly reflects the land–sea contrast.
  • Stale markers: a station is hatched when its latest monthly value is older than September 2026, i.e. it has not reported in the month before the snapshot. All NOAA flask sites are hatched because GML’s public monthly files end in December 2025.
  • Year-on-year change: latest month minus the same month one year earlier.
  • OpenAQ PM₂.₅: OpenAQ's monthly aggregates (mean of daily means from hourly reference-monitor data) are used as published, including months with partial coverage. The anomaly definitions are the same as for CO₂, in µg/m³. Only PM₂.₅ is included from OpenAQ in this snapshot. Monitors are reference-grade only (no low-cost sensors) and were thinned to one per 0.75° cell to keep the page small, so dense urban networks are under-represented. Many monitors have fewer than five years in OpenAQ; sites with fewer than three baseline years are grey. The latest month (October 2026) is partial.

How the basemaps are built

  • OCO-2: daily Fwd_FP / Lite files are read directly; only soundings with xco2_quality_flag = 0 are kept. All good soundings in each 16-day window (one OCO-2 repeat cycle) are averaged on a 0.25° grid (roughly 25 km, close to the width of two OCO-2 swaths); cells with no soundings are transparent. A few daily files that failed to download were skipped, so some windows contain 14–15 days rather than 16; the November 2025 window contains 11 days because of a data gap 6–18 November 2025.
  • Sentinel-3 NDVI: the 300 m Cloud-Optimised GeoTIFFs are read from the CDSE S3 store at their 1/32 overview level (~0.1°, i.e. the mean of 32×32 native pixels), physical NDVI recovered as DN×0.004−0.08 with flagged values (DN>250) dropped. The anomaly is each dekad minus the mean of the same dekad in 2021–2025. Pixels missing in the current dekad are transparent; no-data gaps in the baseline years are ignored in the mean.
  • MODIS NDVI: monthly NDVI images at 0.05° (latest month) or 0.125° (other months) were decoded from the GIBS colour map (0.01 NDVI bins). The anomaly is the month’s NDVI minus the mean of the same month in the five preceding years (four years for April, where one baseline month is missing). Pixels without data in the current month are transparent; ocean and permanent snow/ice are masked in the source.
  • PM₂.₅ model: GEOS-CF v2 monthly means (Oct 2025 – Aug 2026) are read from the server's monthly aggregation; September and October 2026 are means of 6-hourly samples of the hourly v2 analysis. The 2021–2025 baseline is the mean of 6-hourly samples of the GEOS-CF v1 analysis, subsampled to 2°. Anomaly = v2 month − v1 baseline for that calendar month.
  • Fires: detections are plotted as points at 0.01° precision. Low-confidence detections are excluded. Amber points have fire radiative power of 50 MW or more; the slider filters by minimum FRP.

Limitations and substitutions

  • The OCO-2 layer mixes three processing streams. Fwd_FP is a forward (near-real-time) stream that has not had the final bias correction and quality screening of the retrospective Lite product, and v11.2 and v11.3 differ slightly in their calibration; the Lite v11.3r files used for January–May 2026 are the retrospective product. Small offsets of a few tenths of a ppm between windows can come from these differences rather than from the atmosphere. 16-day window means of sun-synchronous, clear-sky soundings are biased toward clear conditions and are not monthly means.
  • Two NDVI products are offered and they are not interchangeable. The default MODIS layer is a monthly composite at 0.05°; the Sentinel-3 layer is a 10-day composite at 300 m (shown at ~0.1°). Their baselines differ in length (MODIS 2020–2025 monthly, CLMS 2021–2025 dekadal) and the CLMS v3 series switched sensor from PROBA-V to Sentinel-3 OLCI in mid-2020, so its 2021–2025 baseline is OLCI-only but earlier years would not be. Dekadal composites are noisier and more cloud-affected than monthly ones, especially in the tropics during the wet season, and the CLMS product has no coverage south of 60°S or north of 80°N. The CLMS series lags about six weeks behind MODIS in this snapshot.
  • Station networks are not harmonised: ICOS values are continuous in-situ monthly means, NOAA values are means of weekly discrete flasks. They are comparable in scale but have different noise characteristics, and ICOS stations are mostly tall towers in Europe while NOAA sites are predominantly remote marine boundary-layer locations.
  • The latest ICOS month (October 2026) is partial and from near-real-time data that has not been through final calibration or quality control; expect revisions.
  • NOAA monthly files in this snapshot end in December 2025, so the NOAA “latest month” is ten months older than the ICOS one.
  • Monthly means at continental stations are strongly influenced by local sources and boundary-layer dynamics; a positive anomaly at one site is not evidence of a regional or global signal.
  • Five-year baselines are short. They reduce the influence of long-term trend but remain sensitive to single anomalous years (for example a previous El Niño inside the baseline window).
  • The PM₂.₅ anomaly compares two model versions: GEOS-CF v2 (current) against a v1 baseline, because the v2 hindcast only begins in 2023. Differences in emissions inventories, chemistry and aerosol schemes between v1 and v2 can produce systematic offsets that are not real changes in air quality; treat the anomaly as indicative. GEOS-CF is a model with assimilated meteorology but no assimilation of surface PM₂.₅ observations, and 2° cells average over strong sub-grid gradients near cities. PM₂.₅ at RH 35 % is a dry-ish mass and is lower than ambient-humidity values reported by many monitors.
  • OpenAQ aggregates what agencies publish; coverage is dense in North America, Europe, India, Japan, Korea and Thailand and sparse across Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia and much of South America. Monitors differ in instrument, siting and QA, and some agencies report provisional values that are later revised or removed.
  • Fire detections are a 24-hour snapshot and include cloud-gap and sun-glint artefacts; the VIIRS NRT product is also subject to later reprocessing.
  • The map uses an equirectangular (EPSG:4326, plate carrée) projection so the gridded rasters register exactly; high latitudes appear horizontally stretched relative to a Mercator map. The world always fills the viewer: zooming out stops where the map’s width or height matches the window, and panning stops at ±90° / ±180°.
  • Nothing on this page is computed live. Dates shown next to each layer are the dates of the underlying data, not of your visit.
